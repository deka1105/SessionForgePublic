25 real situations

When you need to be many people at once

SessionForge gives every identity its own cookie jar, localStorage, and IndexedDB. The same browser, on the same machine, stays signed into the same website as completely different users at the same time — no logging out, no incognito juggling, no second laptop. Here are 25 places that matters.

Work & productivity Development & QA Marketing & social Sales, support & ops Personal & shared devices Privacy & compartmentalization

1–5 Work & productivity

01 Personal + work Google / Microsoft accounts

Keep work and personal Gmail open in parallel — switch calendars and inboxes with one click instead of the Google account-picker dance.

02 Freelancers managing multiple client tenants

A consultant who's an admin on five Slack workspaces, three Notion teams, and two Jira clouds can stop signing out and back in.

03 Multi-tenant SaaS administrators

Cloud / DevOps engineers who admin the same SaaS product (Datadog, Snowflake, Atlassian, Vercel) across several orgs get one identity per org.

04 Open-source maintainers with bot accounts

Run your personal GitHub identity and a bot/CI account side by side without re-authing the GitHub CLI every time.

05 Tax preparers and accountants

A CPA can keep IRS e-services, QuickBooks Online, and bank portals open per client at once — instead of re-logging through 2FA all day.

6–9 Software development & QA

06 Role-matrix testing in one window

Open the same staging URL as admin, editor, viewer, and anonymous in four tabs. Verify access control without four browsers or four profiles.

07 Frontend cookie & session debugging

Reproduce a "works for me / broken for them" bug by holding a working session in one identity and a broken one in another, side by side.

08 Authorized penetration testing

A pentester with a signed scope can run authenticated session A and session B against the target to test horizontal privilege escalation without VM-hopping.

09 Webhook & OAuth flow development

Be the developer account that owns an app and the end user that grants consent — both visible in adjacent tabs while you debug the redirect.

10–13 Marketing, agencies & social media

10 Social-media managers with multiple brand accounts

Agencies managing 5+ Instagram, X, LinkedIn, or TikTok business accounts keep them all logged in — and color-coded — so they never post to the wrong brand.

11 SEO & paid-ads professionals

Run Google Ads, Search Console, and Analytics across many client properties without the "switch account" dropdown timing out.

12 Personalized vs neutral SERP comparison

Open Google in a logged-in identity (personalized) next to a fresh-cookie identity (near-neutral) to see how ranking shifts for the same query.

13 Multi-author publishing platforms

Editors of Medium, Substack, or a multi-author WordPress site can hold the publication's editor login and their personal contributor login at once.

14–17 Sales, support & operations

14 Customer-support agents

Hold a tier-1 agent login and a supervisor / escalation login open together so handoffs don't require a re-auth round trip.

15 CRM admins testing as a sales rep

Open Salesforce / HubSpot as an admin in one tab and as a rep in another — see what they actually see without the impersonation banner breaking the test.

16 E-commerce sellers

Marketplaces like Amazon, Etsy, and eBay let one seller run a personal plus a business account. Keep both open without juggling sign-outs.

17 Legal & paralegal staff

Court e-filing portals (PACER, state ECF) require a separate account per attorney. A paralegal supporting three attorneys can keep all three sessions live.

18–21 Personal life & shared devices

18 Family laptop, one user per identity

On a shared family Mac, each person gets their own identity tile — same browser, same machine, but Mom's Amazon cart never shows up in Dad's recommendations.

19 Streaming household

Netflix, Spotify, and Disney+ profiles tied to one household account, but each member wants their own recommendations and watch history.

20 Online classes for parents + kids

Parent holds the gradebook portal as themselves; kid holds the LMS as the student. One device, no logout cycle when the kid needs to submit.

21 Couples sharing finances

Joint bank account in one identity, personal banking in another. No risk of paying a personal card from the joint account by mistake.

22–25 Privacy & compartmentalization

22 Banking isolated from advertising

Keep your bank, brokerage, and tax tools in an identity that has only ever visited those sites — no third-party ad cookies follow you in.

23 Journalism & source compartmentalization

A journalist can hold an identity that only ever talks to a specific source's secure-drop — never co-mingling with their personal Google or social cookies.

24 Research on filter bubbles

Academics studying recommendation algorithms can keep "primed" identities (one that watched only politics-left, another politics-right) without contaminating their normal account.

25 Travel & regional accounts

Some accounts (Apple ID, Steam, certain streamers) are region-locked. Travelers and expats can keep a US identity and an EU identity in parallel where allowed.
